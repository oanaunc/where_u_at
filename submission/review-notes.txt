THERE IS NO LOGIN. Where U At has no account system. It uses the iCloud
account already signed in on the device. Please make sure the test device is
signed in to iCloud (Settings > [name]) before launching — otherwise the app
shows a "Sign in to iCloud" notice and cannot connect anyone.

IMPORTANT — THE CORE FEATURE NEEDS TWO DEVICES.
Where U At shares location between two people who have each accepted an
invitation. It cannot be demonstrated on a single device with a single Apple
Account. To test the full flow you need two devices signed in to two different
Apple Accounts:

  1. On device A, complete onboarding and allow location.
  2. Tap "Add Someone" > "Share Link" and send the invitation to device B.
  3. On device B, open the link. An invitation screen appears naming the
     sender and stating exactly what accepting means.
  4. Tap "Accept & Share Location".
  5. Both devices now show each other on the Map and People tabs.

WHY THE APP REQUESTS "ALWAYS" LOCATION (guideline 2.5.4)
The app's only purpose is to show the people you have accepted where you are
now. With "While Using" permission, your position stops updating the moment
the app is closed, so the people you share with see a stale pin — the app
cannot do the one thing it exists for. The app therefore requests background
location, and uses it solely to keep the shared position current.

The app is deliberate about this: it asks for "While Using" first, and only
asks for "Always" afterwards, on a screen that explains in plain language what
is gained. If the user declines, the app still works — it says so, on the
Location screen and again on the Privacy tab.

Background location is used for nothing else. There is no advertising, no
analytics, and no third-party SDK of any kind in the app.

CONSENT MODEL (guideline 5.1.1 / 5.1.2)
- Nobody can add themselves to another person's map. Every connection begins
  with an invitation that one person sends and the other explicitly accepts.
- Sharing is mutual by design. There is no one-way watching: if you can see
  someone, they can see you.
- The invitation screen states plainly, before acceptance, that accepting
  means both people can see each other's location.
- Sharing can be paused for everyone or for one person at a time, and the
  Privacy tab lists exactly who can currently see the user.
- Removing a connection deletes the copy of the user's location that the other
  person was able to read.

WHERE THE DATA GOES
Location is written to the user's own private CloudKit database and shared to
specific people using CKShare. Each connection has its own private zone shared
with exactly one participant, read-only — that is what makes per-person
pausing possible. We operate no servers and have no access to any of it.

Only the most recent position is stored; each reading overwrites the last.
No location history is retained anywhere.

Saved places (Home, Work) and arrival-alert rules never leave the user's own
private database and are never shared with anyone, including us.
